## 1. Cohorts, denominators and the model-based reference

### 1.1 Cohort and denominators (all checks)

- **D1 cohort.** The ratio-defined visits of patients with ≥ 2 such visits: 821 patients and 3,528 visits (`hbayes.build_growth_data`, as in D1). Visits are sorted by patient and time.
- **Item sums.** Rebuilt with the Part C reconstruction (`raw_scores.item_visit_table` and `build_visit_frame`). The reconstruction is exact against the frozen panel: 0 mismatches over 5,774 visits.
- **Completeness and labels.** Every D1 visit has all 16 items, and its ratio equals (T/11)/(P/5) exactly, where T is the tremor sum and P the PIGD sum. The D1 statistics recomputed here equal `05_observed.json` (asserted in the script).
- **Label codes.** Labels come from the unchanged thresholds: TD if ratio ≥ 1.15, PIGD if ≤ 0.90, Indeterminate otherwise.
- **S1 (Indeterminate share).** Indeterminate visits / 3,528.
- **S3 (direct-reversion rate, R_D).** The definition is unchanged from D1.
  - A *direct change* is a TD ↔ PIGD label change between consecutive ratio-defined visits j and j+1 of one patient.
  - Its *outcome* is the label at j+2:
    - **revert**: back to the origin label;
    - **stay**: the destination label is kept;
    - **to_Ind**: Indeterminate;
    - **unobserved**: there is no visit j+2.
  - R_D = revert / (revert + stay + to_Ind). Unobserved outcomes are outside the denominator.
  - Direction-specific R_D uses the same definition restricted to TD → PIGD changes (origin TD) or to PIGD → TD changes (origin PIGD).
- **Affected patients (Check 4).** A D1 patient with ≥ 1 *labelled* visit whose PIGD score is 0 (excluded from the ratio sequence because the ratio is undefined).
  - Counts: 236 of the 821 patients, holding 423 excluded visits (378 TD, 45 both-zero Indeterminate).
  - The audit's "382 patients" refers to the whole labelled panel (382 of 1,080 patients), which includes patients that D1 excludes. That figure is re-verified here [O].
- **Uncertainty.**
  - Observed shares and rates: 95 % percentile intervals from a patient bootstrap (B = 2,000, seed 42; whole patients resampled; `raw_scores.patient_bootstrap_sums`).
  - Shape measures in Check 3: B = 1,000.

### 1.2 What "model-based" means here, and what it does not

- **The reference is the frozen D1 *generator*.** That is the full-cohort TT posterior (`fullcohort_tt_draws.npz`, SHA-256 verified against `00_generator.json`) that produced the null cohorts:

  y = β0 + b0 + (β1 + b1) t + σ e,  with b0 ~ N(0, τ0²), b1 = τ1 · t_{ν_s}, e ~ t_{ν_e}.

- **Draws used.** The draws are those of null cohorts 0–99. Each draw index is recovered from that cohort's frozen entropy; no data are generated.
- **How expectations are computed.** By deterministic quadrature over (b0, b1) (40 × 32 nodes) with the exact Student-t CDF for e. Given (b0, b1), a patient's visits are independent, so pair and triplet probabilities are exact products inside the quadrature.
- **Uncertainty.**
  - "Predictive interval" for Indeterminate counts: mean ± 1.96 SD, combining the variation across θ draws with the within-cohort variance, which is computed exactly by quadrature.
  - Ranges for rates and CDF values: 2.5–97.5 % across the 100 θ draws only; cohort sampling variation is **not** included.
- **What it is not.**
  - It is **not** the patient-conditional replicate distribution that D1's z-scores used. Those replicate labels were never saved (§6, M1).
  - The generator is *covariate-blind*. It knows the visit times but not the PIGD sum, so its expected Indeterminate rate is about 0.051 in every PIGD stratum.
  - D1's replicate mean (0.065) is higher because each replicate is centred on the patient's own ratios.

**Validation against the saved null cohorts 0–99** (same draws, realised data) [M]:

| quantity | quadrature expectation | realised mean of the 100 null cohorts |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | 0.05097 | 0.05093 |
| S1, standardised residual (realised − expected) / predicted SD | mean −0.01, SD 1.07 | (calibrated predictive SD) |
| S3 (as a ratio of expectations) | 0.437 | 0.439 |

The quadrature reproduces what the generator actually produced.

**Lattice-catchment variant [H].**
- *Assumption.* The visit's PIGD sum P is fixed, and the tremor sum is a rounded continuous latent value. The Indeterminate label then covers the continuous-ratio interval from the midpoint below its lowest lattice point to the midpoint above its highest (`stage1_checks.lattice_row`). That interval can be wider than the band and shifted: at P = 1 it is [0.68, 1.14]. The generator ratio is labelled with these per-visit cut points.
- *What this ignores.* The dependence between P and the ratio's location, since low-P visits have high ratios.
- *How it is used.* It is a heuristic sensitivity, reported beside the band reference.

## 4. Check 3: residual skew by PIGD level

**Limitation.** A valid TT residual diagnostic is **not possible** from the saved outputs.
- Residuals y − (β + b_i)ᵀx need the patients' posterior random effects b_i. D1 saved only population draws (β, σ², τ0², τ1², ν_s, ν_e) and per-cohort summary statistics. The fold posteriors and b_i were not retained.
- The posterior ν_e is 2.10 (95 % 1.88–2.39), so the TT residual third moment does not exist. Any skew diagnostic must use quantiles, not moment skewness. The earlier skew of 1.20 is a moment skewness of *Gaussian*-model residuals (`experiments/20261015T182129Z_student_t_slope_smoke/results.json`) and does not transfer to the TT fit.
- Nothing was fitted to work around this. The minimum computation needed is M2 (§6).

**Proxy that uses only saved model outputs** (clearly not the TT residual).
- *Definition.* r_ij = (y_ij − β̂1 t_ij) centred on patient i's own mean, with β̂1 the posterior median slope (−0.0813) (`stage1_checks.centred_detrended`).
- *What the generator implies.* r_ij = b1 (t_ij − t̄_i) + σ (e_ij − ē_i). This is symmetric about 0 in law for every visit, so the generator implies:
  - Bowley skew 0;
  - tail asymmetry (Q(1−q) + Q(q)) / (Q(1−q) − Q(q)) = 0;
  - P(r > 0) = 0.5.
- *Sample.* Patients with ≥ 3 visits only (630 patients, 3,146 visits). Two-visit patients (191) give mirror pairs and are symmetric by construction.
- *Stratification.* By the patient's *median* PIGD sum, not the visit's own. Stratifying on the visit's own PIGD sum would select on the outcome.

**Readings.**
- [O] Against the generator's exact symmetry, the proxy is **right-skewed**. The tails are longer upward (tail asymmetry > 0 at 5 % and 1 %) and fewer than half the values lie above the patient mean. The quartile-level (Bowley) skew is about 0, so the asymmetry sits in the tails and in a slight downward shift of the bulk.
- [O] The skew does **not** grow as the PIGD sum falls; the 1–2 stratum has the smallest tail asymmetry. The audit's "growing as the PIGD sum falls" pattern is therefore not seen. The 8+ stratum (10 patients) is uninformative.
- [O] The spread is strongly **denominator-dependent**: the IQR falls from 1.20 to 0.31 across strata, tracking the lattice step. The generator uses one σ for all visits. This heteroscedasticity is a measurement-construction feature that a continuous homoscedastic ratio model cannot represent.
- [H] Right-skewed tails would produce fewer large downward shocks than the generator's symmetric t noise. That would mean fewer transient TD → PIGD dips, matching Check 2's direction. This link is a hypothesis.
- *Caveats.* The proxy includes slope deviations b1(t − t̄), which are also symmetric under the generator. The ratio's floor at 0 (tremor sum 0) contributes to the right skew; that is itself part of measurement construction. The proxy cannot separate patient-level from visit-level asymmetry.

## 7. Recommendation on Stage 2

**Stage 2 (a measurement-faithful integer-score model with no intermediate state) is justified, narrowly specified.**

**Reasons:**
1. **S1.** The excess Indeterminate share is largely explained by how the fixed thresholds sit on the integer lattice (§2.2–2.4), with roughly flat local score distributions. Only a model that generates integer tremor and PIGD sums can reproduce this, or fail to. A continuous-ratio model cannot represent it by construction.
2. **S3.** The direct-reversion deficit is confined to TD → PIGD → TD (§3), and the lattice does not explain it. Separate tremor and PIGD count processes, with a PIGD floor and their own skew, are the natural test of whether measurement construction produces this asymmetry. The ratio's denominator-dependent spread (§4) is a further feature such a model represents and the ratio model does not.
3. **Selection** is weakened as a driver (§5). Stage 2 should still include PIGD = 0 visits, because its target is the full three-state label, not because Stage 1 implicates the exclusion.

**Conditions:**
- Stage 2 needs its own frozen specification and its own calibration and positive control in its units.
- It must first report a PIGD-zero fit check, since every existing count model mis-fits PIGD = 0.
- Pre-specified secondary statistics: stratum-specific S1 and direction-specific R_D.
- It must save per-visit replicate labels.
- The D1 result stays as it is.

**Optional cheaper step before Stage 2: M1.** About 70 s, but it re-evaluates the observed cohort, so approval is needed. It would show whether §2's stratum pattern and §3's direction pattern hold against D1's actual patient-conditional reference.

**A latent-state model is not indicated.**
- Audit §6 criterion 3 fails: Stage 1 does localise the misfit, the S1 part to lattice alignment and the S3 part to one transition direction.
- Criterion 1 (a measurement-faithful model failing) has not been tested.

**Conservative interpretation (unchanged).** Nothing here is evidence for or against a biological intermediate subtype. The checks describe one fitted model, one measurement construction and the observed labels. No clinical-importance claim is made: there is no validated MCID for these item sums.
